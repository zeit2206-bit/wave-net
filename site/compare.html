Why light hit a ceiling

Light passing through plates can only mix and steer. Mixing paint is a good picture: mix in several steps or in one step, you can get the same color. Steps of pure mixing collapse into one. That's called linear.

So 5 plates behaved like 1 clever plate. A standard neuron has an "if-then" step ("if the total is below zero, output zero"), called a nonlinearity. That step stops layers from collapsing, so each layer can build on the last.

Light can mix, but it can't decide.

Why the "light" model still used math

On the laptop, light had to be calculated, which is heavy math. In a real build, nobody calculates: light just travels, the way a thrown ball lands in the right place without solving equations. You do the math once to design the plates; after that, light does the work for free.
