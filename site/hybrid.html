What we tested

After some of the plates, a simulated camera measures how bright each spot is and sends that brightness on as new light. Measuring brightness is a simple if-then-like step. We tried a camera after plate 3, and cameras after plates 2 and 4.

Result
Light only
90.9%
Camera after plate 3
91.5%
Cameras after 2 and 4
91.3%
Standard network
95.3%

It barely helped (+0.6 points, which could even be chance).

Why (best explanation)
  • The camera step is fixed. A neuron's if-then step is tuned during training; this one isn't.
  • The camera throws away half the information: it keeps brightness but loses the phase (the delays).
  • The plates still only steer light. There's no hidden layer of features for the decision step to work on.

Adding any if-then step isn't enough. It needs to be tunable, and everywhere.

Change of direction

Instead of simulating physics, why not use what the computer already is? Its simplest unit is the bit, processed by logic gates. And gates do have the if-then step built in: AND means "only if both". That's the next step.
