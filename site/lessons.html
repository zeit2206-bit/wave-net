Five lessons

Simpler units do work. Light read digits at 91%, logic gates at 95%, with no decimal multiplication.

A network needs an "if-then" step. Light only mixes, so it hit a ceiling. Gates and neurons can decide.

How it's organized matters as much as the unit. Gates went from 185× slower to 2.8× faster just by reorganizing the same work.

The sweet spot is the middle. Not 32 bits, not 1 bit: weights of −1/0/+1 kept accuracy in a tiny model that trains in seconds.

It must be learned that way. Rounding a real model (Llama) to ternary after training destroys it; 4 bits is the safe floor without retraining.

Is any of this new?

No: every main result repeats published work (UCLA's light networks 2018, logic gate networks 2022, ternary networks 2016, BitNet 2024, quantization research). That's good news: our code agrees with the experts. What's ours is the side-by-side comparison of all of them on one laptop.
