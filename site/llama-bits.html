How we measured

We rounded Llama's weights to fewer bits and measured perplexity: how "surprised" the model is by each next word of a normal English text. Lower is better. About 10 means it's choosing among roughly 10 likely words; thousands means it's lost.

We also asked each version to continue "The capital of France is", to see the damage in plain words.

Only the 112 layer matrices were changed; the word table stayed as it was. One test text, one prompt.

What it means
  • Early layers are fragile, late layers are tough. Ternary in layer 0 alone breaks the model; in layer 15 alone it barely matters. My explanation: an error in layer 0 passes through all 15 later layers and grows; an error in the last layer has nowhere to grow.
  • Feed-forward is more fragile than attention. It holds most of the weights and much of the stored knowledge.
  • A model must learn ternary while training. Our digit network was trained ternary and worked. Llama was trained with full precision, so its weights depend on fine detail that −1/0/+1 erases. That's why Microsoft trained BitNet ternary from the start.
Keep in mind

One text and one prompt. Small differences (6-bit vs 8-bit) are noise. The big differences are clear, and they match published research.
