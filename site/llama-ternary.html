Why Llama breaks when made ternary

Our digit network worked with −1/0/+1. Llama, rounded to −1/0/+1, wrote only commas. This page explains why, one problem at a time, and what could fix it.

The short version: rounding throws away most of each weight's detail, the errors pile up through 16 layers, and Llama was never taught to cope with that. Our digit network was.
Problem 1 · Three values can't hold Llama's detail

Llama's weights are small decimals, mostly between −0.02 and +0.02, and each one is slightly different. Ternary forces each one to −1, 0 or +1 times a shared scale. Here are six real-looking weights from one block:

  • 0.021 and 0.009 become the same value, although one was more than twice the other.
  • −0.004 is switched off completely.

Picture a recipe where "21 g of salt" and "9 g of salt" both become "a pinch", and "4 g of sugar" becomes "none". One such change is fine. A billion of them are not.

Problem 2 · The errors pile up through the layers

Llama is a factory with 16 floors. Each floor takes the previous floor's work and adds its own. If every floor makes a 40% mistake, floor 2 works on damaged parts, floor 3 on worse ones, and by floor 16 nothing is left of the original meaning.

Problem 3 · Llama never learned to work with three values

This is the key difference from our digit test.

Our digit networkLlama
How it became ternaryTrained with −1/0/+1 from the first stepTrained with full decimals, then rounded at the end
What training didEach time rounding hurt, training adjusted the other weights to compensateNothing compensated. Its weights depend on fine detail that rounding erased
PictureA choir that practiced with only 3 notes and arranged the song for themA full orchestra's music played on 3 notes, unchanged
Result96.5% (vs 97.3%)Only commas

A network's knowledge is not in single weights, but in how they balance each other. In Llama, many small weights cancel or add up very precisely. Rounding breaks that balance everywhere at once.

Problem 4 · Why not just retrain Llama as ternary?

Retraining would let it re-balance, like our digit network did. But:

  • Size: our digit network had ~118,000 weights; Llama has 1.24 billion, about 10,000× more.
  • Data: digits needed 60,000 small images. Re-balancing Llama in published work takes billions of words.
  • Memory: training needs the weights plus extra copies for the "nudges", roughly 15–20 GB for Llama 1B. That's more than this laptop can spare.
  • Speed: our NumPy Llama takes ~9 s per word just to read. Training is several times heavier per word.

Picture re-teaching a whole orchestra: possible, but it needs a concert hall and months, not a living room and an afternoon.

Problem 5 · Small models are the hardest case

Big models (70B) have many weights doing similar jobs, so losing detail in some is covered by others. A 1B model has fewer spare weights, so each one matters more. Research consistently finds that low-bit conversion hurts small models most.

Next logical step: methods that check errors on real sentences (GPTQ-style), not on weights alone.
