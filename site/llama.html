Getting it
  • You installed Ollama, a free program that runs models locally. We downloaded Llama 3.2 1B (Meta), 1.3 GB.
  • It answers at about 19 tokens per second on this laptop, fully offline.
  • "1B" means about 1 billion parameters: adjustable weights, the same thing as in our digit networks, just many more.

None of these are code or formulas: they're the model's floor plan. Our digit network, by comparison, had 2 floors with trays of 128 slots.

Where 1.24 billion comes from: multiply the sizes. One feed-forward part has 3 matrices of 2,048 × 8,192 ≈ 17 million weights each, about 50 million together. Do that on 16 floors, then add attention and the vocabulary table (128,256 × 2,048).

Weights are tiny (typically 0.01–0.02). About a third are very small, and only 2–4% are large — the pattern that made ternary work on digits.

Is it a "standard" neural network?

Mostly yes. Llama is a transformer. Its feed-forward parts are exactly like our standard digit network: multiply, add, if-then step. The attention parts are extra: they let each word look at the other words (for example, to know what "it" refers to).
